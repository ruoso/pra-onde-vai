Choro harmony: mockups

Each frame is a live page: tap the chords, play the practice. Opened from the file system they work as they are; the design is described in docs/design.md. Sound needs a tap first (browsers start audio only after one).

Choro harmony map

0 · What am I hearing? (find home, then name chords)

Open full size

One screen for a tune you have no chart for. Enter notes; the app fills in whatever is still unknown. With no home yet it ranks home keys: tap one to hear the cadence into it, then 'Make ... home'. With a home it ranks the chords on the map, weighted by how sections open ('Coming from: Nothing') or by what follows the home chord. The rankings are real and recompute as you edit. Hum is simulated (no microphone here): it enters an example phrase.

1 · Leaving home (playable)

Open full size

Seven levels, each adding the functions the corpus uses most after the ones before. Coverage of all chord changes rises 41/46% (major/minor) at level 1 to 91/92% at level 7; tunes with at least 80% of their changes on the board, 2 at level 1 to 261 at level 7. Below level 4 whole tunes are mostly 'played for you', so early levels practise on drills built from the corpus's usual moves. Key moves join at level 3 (relative key) and 5 (parallel, subdominant). The learner here is on level 3; the mastery numbers are an example.

2 · Visiting, or moving in?

Open full size

The reference is one good version, not the only one. Comparing your 9 charts with the corpus's versions of the same tunes: of 202 places they differ, 47% are the same chord with another bass or extension, 29% a passing chord added or left out, 9% a related chord sharing two notes, 5% the same root made into a secondary dominant; only about 5% are genuinely different harmony. So phrase arrivals are marked strictly, and the way between them leniently.

3 · New home

Open full size

When the tune changes key, the app asks before going on: is F a visit, or the new home? 'Make F major home' re-centres the board, as on the map; B continues in F, where the D7 you hear is now the V of ii.

After the run: each phrase with what you got, tap to hear it again. It names the pattern in the misses (here, applied dominants heard as the plain V) and turns it into a short drill in random keys. The numbers are an example run, not computed.
